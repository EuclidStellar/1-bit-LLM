What it will and will not do

It writes grammatical children's stories with dialogue and an ending. It loses track of who is who after a few sentences — characters change name, objects become other objects, a rabbit may fly. That is not the quantization: the full-precision version of this model does it too. It was trained on 20 million tokens, about 11× less than the compute-optimal budget, because the point was measuring what ternary weights cost, not maximizing quality.

It knows no facts, cannot answer questions, and has never seen anything outside simple narrative English.

Its attention window is 256 tokens — what it was trained with. Generation past that uses a ring buffer: the oldest token is dropped as each new one arrives, and because rotary position embeddings encode only the relative distance between tokens, absolute position can run to 4,000 while every offset stays inside the trained range. Nothing is recomputed.

Left alone past its natural ending it degenerates into “The end. The end. The end.” — it was trained on stories of about 227 tokens and has no concept of a longer one. Blocking repeated n-grams makes looping impossible, so it is forced to keep finding something new to say. That is why the output rambles rather than repeats.

So it never stops, but it only ever remembers the last 256 tokens. Ask for 4,096 and it will keep going — drifting, forgetting who anyone was, and quietly contradicting itself. Talking forever while remembering nothing is, in fairness, on brand.

Look at the weights

Ternary weights have three states, so a weight matrix prints as a picture: - is −1, · is zero — no connection — and + is +1. Very few models let you read their parameters directly.







  
The forward pass is plain JavaScript — base-3 unpacking, RMSNorm, RoPE,
  causal attention with a KV cache, squared ReLU, and per-token int8 activation
  quantization. On load it verifies its own logits against the PyTorch reference,
  so if the implementation were wrong you would see it fail above rather than read
  slightly-wrong stories.
